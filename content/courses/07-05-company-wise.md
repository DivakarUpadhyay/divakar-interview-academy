# Overview

Every company type tests the same core skills — fundamentals, problem solving, project knowledge, communication — but with **different emphasis and formats**. Knowing the pattern helps you prepare the right material.

| Company type | Typical emphasis |
|---|---|
| **Indian IT services** (TCS, Infosys, Wipro, HCLTech, Cognizant, Accenture, Capgemini, Tech Mahindra, LTIMindtree) | Fundamentals (OOP, C#, SQL, .NET), project explanation, client communication, flexibility (shifts, relocation, technologies); aptitude tests for freshers |
| **Global product companies** (Microsoft, Amazon, Google, Oracle, Adobe, Salesforce) | Data structures and algorithms, system design (senior), behavioural/values interviews, high hiring bar |
| **Global Capability Centres (GCCs)** (banks, retailers, manufacturers with Indian/Taiwan tech centres) | Mix: strong fundamentals + domain + design + stakeholder skills |
| **Consulting / Big Four** (Deloitte, EY, KPMG, PwC, Accenture consulting) | Technical breadth, client-facing communication, case/scenario questions |
| **Startups** | Practical coding/take-home tasks, ownership, speed, full-stack breadth, culture |
| **Taiwan employers** | Hardware/semiconductor/electronics giants, software companies and foreign firms in Taipei; language requirements vary; work permit eligibility matters for foreigners |

> **Important:** interview processes change frequently and differ by role, level and team. The patterns below are general and based on publicly shared information (company career pages and candidate reports). Always check **recent** candidate experiences (Glassdoor, AmbitionBox, LeetCode Discuss, Blind, PTT Tech_Job for Taiwan) and ask the recruiter about the exact rounds.

# Versions

How company hiring has changed:

| Period | Change |
|---|---|
| 2000s | Mass campus hiring by Indian IT services with written aptitude tests |
| 2010s | Online assessments (AMCAT, CoCubes, HackerRank, Codility); product companies standardise DSA + system design loops |
| 2018–2020 | National qualifier tests (e.g. TCS NQT launched 2018–2019); tiered fresher roles (e.g. TCS Ninja/Digital, Infosys Specialist Programmer, Wipro Elite, Cognizant GenC tiers) |
| 2020–2022 | Fully virtual interviews; hiring boom and high attrition; online proctored tests |
| 2023–2024 | Hiring slowdown, layoffs at many tech companies, longer processes; growth of GCCs in India |
| 2025–2026 | AI-assisted screening, stricter anti-cheating measures in online assessments, more emphasis on practical skills and AI tooling familiarity |

# Lifecycle

**General end-to-end process (experienced hires):**

1. Application / referral / recruiter outreach (LinkedIn, Naukri, Instahyre, 104 in Taiwan).
2. Recruiter screen: experience, skills, notice period, CTC, location.
3. Online assessment or technical screen (coding/MCQ) — more common for freshers and product companies.
4. 1–3 technical rounds (concepts, coding, project deep-dive, design).
5. Managerial / techno-managerial round.
6. HR round (behavioural, salary, joining date).
7. Offer → background verification → onboarding.

**Fresher campus/off-campus process (Indian IT services, typical):** registration → online test (aptitude: quantitative, logical, verbal; programming MCQs; coding; sometimes communication/game-based) → technical interview → managerial/HR interview → offer → training (e.g. initial training programmes before project allocation).

# Module: Indian IT Services Companies

## Lesson: [Basic|High] What IT services interviews focus on

For **experienced .NET developers** (2–10 years), expect:

1. **Self-introduction and current project** — architecture, your role, technologies, team size, client, challenges. Prepare a whiteboard-level diagram.
2. **C# and OOP** — four pillars, abstract class vs interface, overloading vs overriding, `ref`/`out`, `string` vs `StringBuilder`, value vs reference types, `IEnumerable` vs `IQueryable`, async/await, exception handling, collections, delegates/events.
3. **ASP.NET Core / MVC / Web API** — middleware, DI lifetimes, filters, routing, authentication (JWT), model binding/validation, `IActionResult` vs `ActionResult<T>`, REST verbs and status codes.
4. **Entity Framework / ADO.NET** — code-first vs DB-first, migrations, loading strategies, `AsNoTracking`, N+1, transactions; ADO.NET objects.
5. **SQL Server** — joins, second-highest salary, duplicates, CTE, window functions, stored procedure vs function, indexes, `DELETE` vs `TRUNCATE`, normalisation, performance.
6. **Frontend basics** if full-stack — JavaScript, Angular/React fundamentals.
7. **Azure/DevOps basics** — App Service, Functions, Key Vault, CI/CD pipelines, Git.
8. **Coding** — simple programs (string reverse, palindrome, duplicates, Fibonacci, LINQ queries), sometimes on a shared editor or notepad.
9. **Client-facing/scenario questions** — handling production issues, changing requirements, working in shifts, communicating delays.
10. **HR** — notice period, relocation, salary, bond (for some fresher roles).

## Lesson: [Basic|Medium] Fresher hiring patterns (typical, verify current details)

- **TCS** — the **TCS NQT** (National Qualifier Test) is used for fresher hiring, with role tiers commonly known as **Ninja** and **Digital** (and **Prime** for the highest tier in recent drives). Interviews typically combine technical, managerial and HR discussions.
- **Infosys** — fresher roles include Systems Engineer and higher tiers such as **Digital Specialist Engineer** and **Specialist Programmer**; coding competitions such as **HackWithInfy** have been used as a hiring channel.
- **Wipro** — **Elite NLTH** (National Level Talent Hunt) for freshers; online assessment + interviews.
- **Cognizant** — fresher tracks named **GenC**, **GenC Next**, **GenC Pro/Elevate** (names change between years).
- **Accenture** — online assessments covering cognitive abilities, technical MCQs, coding and a communication assessment, followed by an interview.
- **Capgemini** — has used game-based aptitude tests alongside pseudo-code, English and technical assessments.
- **HCLTech, Tech Mahindra, LTIMindtree** — online assessment + technical + HR, with role-specific variants.

Common fresher topics: aptitude, any one programming language, OOP, DBMS/SQL, OS basics, computer networks basics, final-year project, and communication.

## Lesson: [Intermediate|High] Project explanation that impresses

Structure (3–5 minutes):

1. **Business context** — client/domain, users, problem solved.
2. **Architecture** — frontend → API → services → database → integrations; hosting (Azure/on-prem); draw it.
3. **Your role** — modules you owned, decisions you made.
4. **Tech stack** — and *why* (e.g. "Dapper for reporting queries because EF generated inefficient SQL").
5. **A challenge** — problem → investigation → solution → measurable result.
6. **Process** — Agile/Scrum, CI/CD, code reviews, testing.
7. **What you'd improve.**

Expect drill-down questions on anything you mention — only mention what you can explain deeply.

# Module: Global Product Companies

## Lesson: [Intermediate|High] Amazon: Leadership Principles and the loop

- Amazon evaluates candidates against its **16 Leadership Principles** (Customer Obsession, Ownership, Invent and Simplify, Are Right A Lot, Learn and Be Curious, Hire and Develop the Best, Insist on the Highest Standards, Think Big, Bias for Action, Frugality, Earn Trust, Dive Deep, Have Backbone; Disagree and Commit, Deliver Results, Strive to be Earth's Best Employer, Success and Scale Bring Broad Responsibility).
- Typical SDE process: online assessment (coding + work-style questions) → interview loop (commonly 4–5 interviews) mixing coding/DSA, system design (for SDE II and above) and behavioural questions mapped to LPs. One interviewer is often a **Bar Raiser** — an experienced interviewer from outside the hiring team who helps maintain the hiring bar.
- Prepare **2 STAR stories per principle** with metrics; expect deep follow-ups ("What exactly did you do? What data did you use?").

## Lesson: [Intermediate|High] Microsoft and Google: what to expect

**Microsoft** (typical, varies by team): recruiter screen → one or more technical screens → a loop of around 4–5 interviews covering coding (DSA), design (system/object-oriented design for experienced roles), and behavioural/collaboration questions. Culture emphasis: **growth mindset**, collaboration, customer focus. Knowledge of Azure/.NET helps for many teams but isn't always required.

**Google** (typical): recruiter screen → technical phone/video screen → several interviews (often 4–5) covering coding/algorithms, system design (for experienced roles) and a **Googleyness & Leadership** behavioural interview. Interview feedback is reviewed by a **hiring committee**, followed by team matching. Coding is usually in a shared doc/editor without IDE autocomplete — practise writing clean code by hand.

Common across these companies: arrays/strings, hash maps, two pointers, sliding window, trees/graphs (BFS/DFS), heaps, dynamic programming (more at Google), and design topics (URL shortener, rate limiter, chat system, news feed, distributed cache).

## Lesson: [Intermediate|Medium] System design basics for product-company rounds

Framework (45 minutes):

1. **Requirements** — functional (features) and non-functional (scale, latency, availability, consistency). Ask questions.
2. **Estimates** — users, requests/second, storage growth.
3. **API design** — main endpoints.
4. **High-level design** — clients → load balancer → API services → cache → database → queues → workers → CDN.
5. **Data model** — SQL vs NoSQL, keys, indexes, partitioning/sharding.
6. **Deep dive** — the hardest part (e.g. generating unique short IDs, fan-out for feeds, rate limiting algorithm).
7. **Bottlenecks and trade-offs** — caching strategy, replication, consistency, failure handling, monitoring.

Classic problems: URL shortener, rate limiter, notification system, chat (WebSockets), news feed, file storage (Dropbox), ride-hailing matching, payment system (idempotency), search autocomplete.

# Module: Consulting, GCCs, Startups and Taiwan

## Lesson: [Intermediate|Medium] Consulting firms and GCCs

- **Big Four / consulting** (Deloitte, EY, KPMG, PwC, Accenture consulting): technical breadth plus client communication; scenario questions ("The client is unhappy with delivery — what do you do?"), estimation, presenting solutions; sometimes case-style discussions. Domain knowledge (banking, insurance, tax, audit, ERP) is valued.
- **GCCs** (Global Capability Centres of banks, retailers, insurers, manufacturers in Bengaluru, Hyderabad, Pune, Chennai and elsewhere): rounds similar to product companies but often more practical — strong C#/.NET/SQL, design, cloud, code quality and stakeholder management with global teams; behavioural rounds focused on ownership and collaboration across time zones.

## Lesson: [Intermediate|Medium] Startups

- Practical: **take-home assignments** (build a small API/UI in a few hours), live coding on real-world problems, code review exercises, product thinking.
- They look for ownership, speed, breadth (frontend + backend + DevOps), learning ability and comfort with ambiguity.
- Ask about: funding/runway, business model, team size, on-call expectations, equity (vesting, cliff, valuation), and work hours.
- Show you can ship: GitHub projects, deployed demos, measurable impact.

## Lesson: [Intermediate|Medium] Interviewing with Taiwan employers

- Major employers include semiconductor and electronics companies (TSMC, MediaTek, Foxconn/Hon Hai, ASUS, Acer, Quanta, Delta Electronics), software and internet companies (Trend Micro, Appier, Gogoro, LINE Taiwan/LY Corporation, Shopee Taiwan, KKCompany), and foreign multinationals' Taipei offices (Microsoft, Google Taiwan hardware teams, Amazon Web Services, NVIDIA and others).
- **Language**: many local companies interview and work in Mandarin; English-only roles exist mainly in multinationals, startups with international teams and some R&D centres — filter job posts (e.g. "English" on 104/Yourator/CakeResume/LinkedIn).
- **Process**: online application → HR phone screen → technical test or take-home → 1–3 interviews (team lead, manager, sometimes department head) → offer with monthly salary + bonus discussion.
- **Work authorisation**: foreign professionals need an employer-sponsored work permit or a **Taiwan Employment Gold Card**; be ready to state your status clearly.
- **Culture tips**: punctuality, politeness and humility; prepare a short self-introduction; bring/attach transcripts or certificates if asked; follow up through the recruiter.

# Interview

## [Beginner|High] (IT services) Explain your current project and your role in it.

Structure: business context → architecture → your role → tech stack → one challenge with a measurable result → process. Example: "I work on a loan-origination system for a European bank. It's an Angular frontend calling ASP.NET Core Web APIs, with SQL Server and Azure Service Bus for integration with the credit-scoring service, hosted on Azure App Service. I own the document-verification module and the reporting APIs. A key challenge was slow PDF generation timing out under load; I moved it to a background worker with Service Bus and blob storage, cutting API response time from 25 seconds to under 1 second. We work in two-week sprints with Azure DevOps pipelines and mandatory code reviews."

## [Beginner|High] (IT services) Why do you want to join a services company like ours?

Mention exposure to multiple clients, domains and technologies; structured training and certifications; global delivery experience; career paths (technical or managerial); and the company's specific strengths (a major client in your domain, a cloud or AI practice, a location). Example: "Working with global clients across banking and retail would broaden my domain knowledge. Your Microsoft cloud practice is strong, and your certification programmes would help me move towards solution architecture."

## [Beginner|High] (IT services) Are you comfortable working in different shifts, technologies or locations?

Answer honestly with any constraints: "Yes, I'm comfortable with overlap with UK or US hours and occasional on-call support. I'm primarily a .NET developer but I've also worked on Angular and SSIS, and I'm open to learning what the project needs. I'd prefer Pune or Bengaluru, but I'm open to discussing other locations." Ask how often shifts rotate.

## [Beginner|High] (Freshers) What happens in online assessments like TCS NQT or similar tests, and how do you prepare?

These tests typically cover quantitative aptitude, logical reasoning, verbal ability, programming fundamentals and one or two coding problems, all under time pressure, with proctoring. Prepare with timed practice (aptitude books/sites, previous-year style questions), master one language for coding (input/output handling, strings, arrays), revise OOP, DBMS/SQL and OS basics, and read the official registration page for the current pattern, since sections change between years.

## [Intermediate|High] (Amazon) Tell me about a time you disagreed with your manager or team. (Have Backbone; Disagree and Commit)

"S: My manager wanted to ship a reporting feature by computing aggregates on every request. T: I believed it would overload the database at month-end. A: I gathered data — query costs and expected month-end traffic — and proposed pre-aggregating nightly with a fallback for today's data. He disagreed because of the timeline, so we agreed on a quick load test; the on-request version exceeded our latency target by 4×. R: We implemented my approach in 2 extra days; month-end reports stayed under 2 seconds. Had the test gone the other way, I'd have committed fully to his design." Show data, respect and commitment.

## [Intermediate|High] (Amazon) Tell me about a time you obsessed over a customer. (Customer Obsession)

Pick a story where you went beyond the ticket to understand the user's real problem: "Customers kept calling support about failed payments. Instead of just fixing the error message, I analysed 3 months of logs and found 40% of failures came from one bank's timeout. I added retries with idempotency keys and a clear status page for pending payments. Support calls on payments dropped by 60%." Quantify customer impact.

## [Intermediate|High] (Amazon) Tell me about a time you had to dive deep to solve a problem. (Dive Deep)

"Our API had random 5-second spikes. Dashboards showed nothing obvious, so I collected traces and found the spikes coincided with Gen 2 garbage collections caused by large JSON payloads (over 85 KB) landing on the Large Object Heap. I switched to streaming serialisation and paginated the responses. P99 latency dropped from 5.2 s to 300 ms." Show detailed investigation and data.

## [Intermediate|High] (Microsoft/Google) How do you approach a coding interview problem?

Clarify requirements and constraints (input size, edge cases), talk through examples, state a brute-force solution with its complexity, optimise (hash map, two pointers, sorting, BFS/DFS, DP), write clean code while explaining, test with examples and edge cases, and state time and space complexity. Communicate throughout — interviewers evaluate problem solving and communication, not just the final answer.

## [Intermediate|High] (Product companies) Design a URL shortener.

Requirements: shorten URLs, redirect fast, custom aliases, expiry, analytics; read-heavy (say 100:1). API: `POST /urls` → short code; `GET /{code}` → 301/302 redirect. ID generation: base62 encoding of a unique ID (from a distributed ID generator or a pre-allocated range per server) to avoid collisions, or random codes with collision checks. Storage: key-value or SQL table (code PK → long URL, created, expiry, owner); cache hot codes in Redis; CDN/edge for redirects. Scale: stateless app servers behind a load balancer, DB replication/sharding by code, async analytics via a queue. Discuss 301 vs 302 (caching vs analytics), abuse/malware checks and rate limiting.

## [Intermediate|Medium] (Consulting) The client says your team's delivery quality is poor. How do you respond?

Listen first and gather specifics (which defects, which releases, impact). Acknowledge and share a data-backed analysis (defect trends, root causes — requirements gaps, rushed testing, environment issues). Propose a concrete improvement plan: stronger acceptance criteria, automated tests and code-quality gates, a UAT checklist, release readiness reviews, and weekly quality reporting. Agree on measurable targets and follow up. Keep it professional and collaborative, never defensive.

## [Intermediate|Medium] (Startups) You get a 4-hour take-home: build a small task-management API. What do you prioritise?

Read the requirements carefully and clarify assumptions in a README. Deliver a working, clean core first: a well-structured ASP.NET Core API with validation, error handling (ProblemDetails), persistence (EF Core with SQLite for easy setup), and a few meaningful tests. Then add polish: Docker/compose to run it, OpenAPI docs, seed data, and notes on trade-offs and what you'd do with more time (auth, pagination, CI). Commit in small, clear steps. Quality and clarity beat feature count.

## [Intermediate|Medium] (Taiwan) Why do you want to work in Taiwan, and how do you handle the language?

Give genuine reasons: Taiwan's strong technology and semiconductor ecosystem, the specific company's products, career growth, and quality of life. Be honest about language: "I work professionally in English and my team communication would be in English; I'm learning Mandarin (currently basic) and I'm comfortable working with bilingual colleagues and tools." State your work-permit or Gold Card status clearly. Only apply for roles whose language requirements you can meet.

## [Advanced|Medium] (GCC) How would you work effectively with a team spread across India, Europe and the US?

Agree overlap hours for synchronous meetings and rotate inconvenient slots fairly; use asynchronous communication well (clear written updates, design docs, recorded demos, well-written tickets); document decisions (ADRs, wiki); use follow-the-sun handovers with checklists for support; be explicit about time zones in deadlines; build relationships with video calls and occasional visits; and respect cultural differences in communication styles.

## [Scenario|High] You have interviews with a services company and a product company in the same week. How do you prepare differently?

Common base: strong project explanation, C#/.NET/SQL fundamentals, behavioural stories. Services company: emphasise breadth (C#, ASP.NET Core, EF, SQL, Azure basics), client communication, flexibility, and classic coding programs. Product company: practise DSA problems by pattern (timed), system design for your level, and values-based behavioural stories (e.g. Amazon LPs), and write code without IDE help. Schedule the harder one later if possible, and use one as practice for the other.

## [Scenario|High] You failed a round at a company you really wanted. What do you do next?

Ask the recruiter politely for feedback (not always shared). Write down every question you were asked the same day and identify gaps. Create a focused plan (e.g. 4 weeks of DSA patterns or system design) and practise with mock interviews. Many companies allow re-applying after a cooling-off period (commonly around 6–12 months, depending on the company) — check the policy and re-apply stronger. Meanwhile, keep interviewing elsewhere for practice.

## [Scenario|Medium] A recruiter from a big brand offers a role with a lower title and similar pay. Should you accept?

Compare beyond the title: the actual responsibilities and level mapping (titles differ between companies — a "Senior Engineer" in one may equal "Engineer II" in another), learning opportunities, brand value for your career, team and manager, tech stack, stability, and growth path to the next level. Ask how levels map and what promotion timelines look like. Negotiate the level if your experience justifies it, with examples of scope you've handled.
